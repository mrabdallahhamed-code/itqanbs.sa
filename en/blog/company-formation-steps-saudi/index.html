Your Complete Guide to Setting Up a Company in Saudi Arabia, Step by Step

From choosing the legal form to issuing the unified Commercial Registration via the Business Platform, and your obligations after incorporation.

Startups September 2026

Setting up a company in Saudi Arabia today is an almost entirely digital process, handled through the Business Platform of the Saudi Business Center, without needing to visit a ministry in person in most cases. This guide walks you through the practical steps in order, and the obligations that begin the moment your registration is issued.

Step One: Choosing the Right Legal Form

Before taking any action, decide on the legal form that best suits your project:

  • Sole Proprietorship: suited to small projects run by a single Saudi national, and the simplest and fastest to set up.
  • Limited Liability Company (LLC): the best option when there is more than one partner, or when you want to separate the company's financial liability from its owners' personal liability — the most common choice among startups and family businesses.

Non-GCC residents cannot open a sole proprietorship directly in their own name under the current system; if you are a foreign investor, your path begins with investment registration at the Ministry of Investment first.

Step Two: Registering via the Business Platform

Issuing a Commercial Registration is now entirely electronic via the Business Platform, and usually involves five main steps:

  1. Log in to the platform with your digital identity.
  2. Choose the economic activity that matches your business from the approved list.
  3. Notarise the incorporation contract electronically, specifying the partners, capital and share allocation.
  4. All partners electronically sign the contract and articles of association using their digital identity.
  5. Pay the prescribed fees and receive your digital Commercial Registration immediately upon payment.
In practice, the actual issuance steps on the platform can take only minutes once your information is complete and accurate — but good preparation (choosing the right activity, structuring the share allocation) is what determines how smoothly the process runs.

The Unified Commercial Registration: What's New?

The current system is built on a single, unified Commercial Registration covering your activity across the entire Kingdom — meaning one registration covers every branch and region, without the separate sub-registrations previously required for each one.

After the Registration Is Issued: Your First 30 Days

Issuing the Commercial Registration isn't the end of the process — it marks the start of new statutory obligations that must be completed within 30 days of issuance:

  • Opening the establishment's file with the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development.
  • Registering with the General Organization for Social Insurance (GOSI).
  • Tax and zakat registration with the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority (ZATCA).

Missing this deadline without completing registration can expose the business to fines or delays in issuing work visas later on.

Renewal and Ongoing Compliance

The Commercial Registration requires mandatory annual renewal; late renewal triggers an immediate fine of SAR 1,000, in addition to suspension of the establishment's services with the Ministry of Human Resources and a freeze on its bank accounts until renewal is completed. Registration details must also be confirmed within 12 months of issuance; failing to confirm within 90 days leads to automatic suspension of the registration.

Common Mistakes When Setting Up

One of the most common mistakes is assuming the Commercial Registration alone is enough to start operating, when some activities require additional licences (municipal, health, industrial) before actual operations begin. Many partners also overlook the 30-day deadline for GOSI and zakat registration, exposing themselves to violations that are easily avoided with advance planning.
